PAIL:Vision-Guided Predictive Action Imitation Learning with Discrete Latent Encoding for Multitasking Robots

Sai Navaneet*, Lingala Manisha, SangMoon lee,
Kyungpook National University, Daegu , South Korea
Paper Code arXiv

Abstract

We present Vision-Guided Predictive Action Imitation Learning with Discrete Latent Encoding for Multitasking Robots (PAIL), a novel approach designed to overcome critical challenges in imitation learning. Unlike traditional methods that rely on Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), PAIL employs discrete latent representations to enhance efficiency and reduce memory usage, while maintaining high-quality data encoding. By leveraging quantized embeddings, PAIL avoids redundant continuous optimization, making it more resource-efficient. Additionally, PAIL incorporates a Transformer-based architecture with cross-attention mechanisms, enabling it to capture long-range dependencies effectively in sequential decision-making tasks. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that PAIL surpasses traditional VAE-based methods, showcasing its potential for scalable and resource-efficient robotic learning.

PAIL



An overview of the PAIL framework, illustrating the process from raw image inputs to action sequence outputs. First, sequences of images are processed by a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to extract low-level spatial features. These features are then passed to a Transformer network, which captures temporal dependencies and contextual information across image frames. A Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) further encodes the resulting high-level representation into a discrete latent space, denoted as $Z_i^q$. Finally, this quantized latent representation is decoded into a corresponding sequence of actions, enabling the system to learn robust policies from visual observations.
